How the peg pyramid works

The board is a triangle of pegs arranged in staggered rows. A ball starts at the top, then moves left or right at each peg until it reaches a pocket at the bottom. More rows mean more possible paths, so the final landing point becomes harder to predict, even though each step still follows the same basic logic.

That pattern is why the game behaves like a probability chart rather than a skill game. The middle pockets are hit more often, while the outer pockets appear less frequently and usually carry larger payouts. Six words worth knowing

Pocket
The slot at the bottom of the board where the ball finally lands and the multiplier is assigned.
Rows

A setting that changes how rewards are spread across the board.

Provably fair
A system that lets players verify that the outcome was fixed before the round began.

The degree of ups and downs in results, especially when using high-risk settings.
